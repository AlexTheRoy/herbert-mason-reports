03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally.
  • Wind and tornadoNRI strong wind and tornado risk: the same build for straight-line wind and tornadoes. Together with hail these are the roof and siding claims.
  • FloodNRI inland flood risk: river and flash flooding times the value exposed. The tier rules also use the share of a ZIP's residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for inland flooding, which picks out the river and floodplain neighborhoods.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (roof age, elevation, distance to a river) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards. Lake Michigan coastal flooding and winter weather are in the spreadsheet but not scored.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeComposite score 90+, or 75%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for inland flooding with a ZIP flood score of 85+
  • HighComposite score 82+, or 50%+ of residents in high-flood tracts
  • ModerateComposite score 75+, or 25%+ of residents in high-flood tracts
  • LowEverything else. The cut points are set within Illinois and Northwest Indiana, so Low still means a real tornado and wind exposure.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme marks about one home in 11 (8.8% of housing): the most storm-exposed downstate ZIPs and the ZIPs where most residents live in high-flood tracts.

Home campaign: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets a flood and roof review · High tier gets a water backup and deductible review

Flood and roof review · Extreme · 301 ZIPs · 8.8% of housing Water backup and deductible review · High · 379 ZIPs · 19.9% No change · 764 ZIPs · 71.3%
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeFlood and roof review. Ask roof age and material, explain the wind and hail deductible, and offer a separate flood policy and the water backup endorsement on every quote. A standard homeowners policy does not cover flood.
  • HighWater backup and deductible review. Ask about the basement and sump pump, quote water backup coverage, and walk through the wind and hail deductible before the spring storm season.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; water backup is still worth offering on any home with a basement.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
These tiers describe where a conversation matters most, not where to stop advertising. Many of your best south-suburban ZIPs are High or Extreme for flood, and those homeowners are the ones who most need the coverage. We do not recommend excluding any ZIP on weather risk. No geo-targeting changes have been made.

Storm and flood in the conversation

Questions that make an Illinois home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Water backupMost Chicago-area homes have a basement. A sewer or sump backup during a heavy storm is not covered by a standard policy without the water backup endorsement; offering it on every quote is the simplest way to stand out.
  • FloodRiver and overland flooding needs a separate flood policy. In High and Extreme flood ZIPs, raising it at quote time shows the client you know their area and adds a second policy to the household.
  • Roofs and wind75% of Chicago-area housing sits in ZIPs scoring 80+ nationally for tornado. Roof age and material belong in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • DeductiblesWhere a separate wind and hail deductible applies, explaining it at quote time avoids a surprise after the first storm claim.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, strong wind, tornado, inland flooding, coastal flooding, winter weather), all 3,263 Illinois and 1,693 Indiana tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Illinois and Indiana are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020-2024.
  • LeadsAll 1,343 GOAL Home lead records for this account (all three Home campaigns), since launch, Jul 24, 2025 to Oct 29, 2026, counted by the ZIP on each lead; sold means the lead is marked sold in GOAL from your status uploads (30). 1,283 leads matched a populated ZIP in the study area; 60 used ZIPs elsewhere in Indiana or in Michigan. Only counts by ZIP are used. Call topics are counts of inbound call summaries that mention each topic.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Elevation, the basement, the roof and the sump pump decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesMason-IL-ZIP-Data-10-29-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report and your Home lead and sold counts by ZIP.
